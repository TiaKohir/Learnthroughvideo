# The Big Three: BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street

MUTUAL FUNDS & ETFS · By IBF · Published February 27, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026 · 7 min read · Data as of: dated 2025 company and 2026 product examples; hypothetical cost budget

## The Bottom Line

BlackRock, Vanguard and State Street are major index-fund sponsors with different businesses, ownership arrangements and products. Their corporate asset totals, fund holdings and voting authority are different measures. Compare actual funds, total costs, tax consequences and voting policies rather than assuming that one sponsor's scale guarantees the best client outcome.

## What "Big Three" Means Here

The term identifies BlackRock, Vanguard and State Street in discussions of index investing and corporate governance. It is not a claim that these are the three largest organizations under every definition of global asset management. Rankings can change when the researcher counts mutual funds, ETFs, separate accounts or assets under administration.

For a dated example, BlackRock reported approximately $14.0 trillion in AUM at year-end 2025. State Street reported approximately $5.7 trillion in AUM, including assets for which it acts solely as marketing agent. Their company totals include activities beyond the U.S. equity index funds that feature in many concentration discussions. Adding corporate totals and dividing by U.S. ETF assets would not produce a meaningful domestic sponsor share.

Fund shareholders have an economic interest in the fund's assets; the adviser does not simply own those client investments for its own corporate benefit. Voting authority, beneficial ownership and corporate control also require separate definitions. Treating those terms as synonyms can overstate what a fund sponsor's reported position means.

## BlackRock: A Broad Investment and Technology Business

BlackRock's 2009 acquisition of Barclays Global Investors brought iShares into the organization. Its business includes index and active investments, public and private markets, cash management and technology services. A company AUM figure therefore does not measure the assets of iShares alone.

Breadth can offer advisors a range of exposures and tools, but using several funds from one organization does not ensure that they provide distinct economic risks. Inspect holdings, benchmarks, legal structures and service arrangements. A sector ETF and a broad-market ETF can overlap substantially even when their names emphasize different mandates.

Corporate scale may support systems and distribution. It does not establish the spread, depth or tracking of a particular product. Examine the actual ETF and proposed order rather than importing the trading characteristics of a flagship product into every fund bearing the same brand.

## Vanguard: Ownership Structure and the Actual Class

Vanguard's U.S. investor-owned structure means its funds own Vanguard and the funds are owned by their shareholders. That structure can support its low-cost approach. It does not prove that every available Vanguard class has the lowest fee, that competitors cannot offer a lower price or that all possible service incentives are permanently resolved.

Compare the class the client can actually buy. A mutual-fund class, ETF class and institutional class can share an underlying portfolio while having different expenses, purchase requirements and trading mechanics. A whole-portfolio asset total should not be described as the assets of one ticker.

Qualifying in-kind ETF redemptions can reduce fund-level realized gains. That does not make every shareholder transaction tax-free. Investors may owe tax on distributions or on selling appreciated shares, and a tax-advantaged account has different considerations from a taxable brokerage account.

Vanguard's principal ETF-share-class patent expired in 2023. Patent expiration and regulatory permission are separate questions. The SEC granted Dimensional conditional multi-class relief in November 2025; other arrangements must satisfy their applicable legal requirements. Do not describe the patent as still expiring or assume that a shared-portfolio structure guarantees a particular after-tax result.

## State Street: More Than One S&P 500 Product

State Street Investment Management, formerly State Street Global Advisors, offers both SPY and the lower-cost SPYM as distinct ways to obtain S&P 500 exposure. A comparison of sponsors that includes only SPY can miss an important within-sponsor alternative.

SPY's unit-investment-trust structure differs from open-end ETFs in dividend handling and portfolio-management flexibility. That structural distinction is relevant, but it does not by itself establish a guaranteed investor-return ranking. Tracking, expenses, distributions, taxes and execution costs all contribute to a realized result.

State Street's July 2026 comparison lists expenses of 0.0945% for SPY and 0.02% for SPYM, with its displayed market data dated March 31, 2026. Those are dated disclosure examples, not a current quotation or an instruction to switch. Check the prospectus and live trading conditions for an actual order.

## Show the Cost Question You Are Answering

On an unchanged average $500,000 balance for one year, a 0.0945% expense ratio represents $472.50 and a 0.02% ratio represents $100. The difference is $372.50. This is simple annual expense arithmetic under a constant-balance assumption, with no other costs or taxes. It does not forecast the ending values of two investments.

An illustrative new $100,000 purchase makes the execution issue visible. Suppose Fund A's assumed annual expense is 0.02% and its total entry-plus-exit trading cost is 0.10% of the unchanged position value. Suppose Fund B's expense is 0.0945% and its corresponding round-trip cost is 0.01%. Assume a one-year holding period, identical gross economic exposure, zero gross return, no other costs or taxes, and calculate both quoted costs on the original $100,000. Total modeled costs are $120 for A and $104.50 for B.

This deliberately simple cost budget shows that a lower expense ratio need not produce the lower total one-year cost. It is not a model of actual SPYM or SPY spreads or returns. Different trade sizes, holding periods, prices or tax consequences can change the comparison. An existing taxable position requires the sale-tax funding and no-change account to be included as well.

## Governance Needs a Defined Population

Academic research on the Big Three examines concentrated holdings and delegated voting. One influential 2017 paper used 2015 data to analyze the three together as a hypothetical block. A historical combined-block result is not evidence that one firm individually is now the largest shareholder in the same proportion of companies.

Voting statistics also depend on the sample, proposal type and weighting. A manager-average support rate is different from the share of all corporate votes cast. Support for management resolutions and support for shareholder resolutions describe different sets of proposals; they are not complementary percentages that should sum to one hundred.

Read each fund's current voting policy, disclosures and any eligible investor-choice arrangements. A pass-through choice may apply only to particular funds or accounts. It is not equivalent to every investor personally voting every underlying share, and it does not establish that all three firms organize their voting teams in the same way.

## Liquidity and Competition Remain Product Questions

ETF liquidity depends on underlying markets and creation/redemption activity as well as secondary-market volume. In stress, reported NAVs can use less current underlying prices than exchange trading. A discount needs interpretation before it is described as a loss relative to a price an investor could actually obtain for all underlying holdings.

Sponsor dependence is a reasonable operational due-diligence question. It does not justify assuming an unmodeled systemic collapse or claiming that three sponsor names provide three independent custodial and trading infrastructures. Check actual service providers and contingency arrangements.

The active-passive arithmetic also needs its assumptions. In Sharpe's simplified framework, define the same market for both groups, let passive investors hold it in market-cap weights, and compare dollar-weighted active and passive returns in a fixed-market setting. Under those assumptions their gross returns coincide and the net difference reflects active costs minus passive costs; its sign depends on which costs are higher. Changing index constituents, outstanding shares and actual implementation trades complicate applying the simplified identity to real funds. This is not a prediction that every active fund loses or that a category label identifies a future winner.

## The Client Conversation

"Are these index funds interchangeable?"

"They may target similar exposure, but we should compare the actual class, trading costs, taxes, account access and voting policy. Let's include keeping what you own before deciding whether a different fund would improve the result."

## Key Takeaways

- Keep company AUM, client holdings, voting authority and control separate.
- Compare actual available products rather than selecting from corporate scale alone.
- Ownership and tax structures can matter without guaranteeing the lowest client cost.
- Include State Street's different S&P 500 products in a relevant comparison.
- Match governance statistics to their dates, samples and weighting methods.
- Evaluate total cost and feasible implementation before recommending a change.
